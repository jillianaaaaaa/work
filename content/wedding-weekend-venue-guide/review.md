## Verdict

**Approve with changes.** With the byline hidden, this still reads as Wedded Wonderland: every property claim is dated to an operator document, the recommendations come with limits ("up to 90 guests sleeping on site", "an evening of up to 100 guests"), and the rebuilt H2 8 says something no ranking page does, which is that operators' own documents disagree. The writer's checklist ticks hold on mechanics (no em dashes, no colons in prose, no "from X to Y", every paragraph 2 or 3 sentences, banned words clear). The ticks miss five things: a disclosure line that overstates the Rosewood Elite position, one Vogue fact stretched past its source, one unverified comparative, one invented generalisation, and a season gloss that is wrong. Changes 1 to 5 must be made before the piece goes to Jillian. Changes 6 to 12 are line-level polish.

## The three questions

1. **Why does this deserve to be featured?** Multi-day weddings are independently reported (Axios, US) and tied to our reader by Virtuoso's exclusive-use figure, and they change the venue decision itself.
2. **What can our reader learn here that a generic search result would not give them?** How to read a venue's actual terms: tiered buy-outs, beds against guests, rooms used in sequence and house rules, each shown with a dated operator fact, plus the warning that brochures and websites conflict, so terms must be confirmed in writing.
3. **Does this strengthen Wedded Wonderland's authority and point of view?** Yes. Two recommendations with stated limits and two principle examples read as judgement. Packages, Concierge, Studio and the honeymoon cluster are woven into the copy rather than added at the end.
