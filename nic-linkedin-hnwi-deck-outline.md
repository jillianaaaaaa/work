## Slide 3: Target Audience Behavior

**Visual Content:**

- They read quietly: few public likes; interest shows as profile visits and DMs
- They reward substance: real deals, names, dates and numbers
- They vet before they connect: headline, About, Featured, mutuals
- They follow trusted names: peers who comment, tag or share act as the introduction
- Benchmark fact: 150 to 300 words and 7 to 8am posts win across all 5 accounts

**Speaker Notes (For Jillian):**

This slide is about how this audience actually behaves, because it changes how we measure success.

First, they read quietly. High-net-worth readers rarely like or comment publicly. They read, they look at your profile, and if something lands, they message you directly or mention you to someone. So a post with modest likes can still be doing its job. This is a working assumption we'll validate with your analytics, specifically profile views and who is viewing.

Second, they reward substance. They see a lot of polished self-promotion and they skip it. What earns their attention is specifics: a real deal, a named place, a date, a number, and a lesson that only someone who was in the room could share. JJ's benchmark data backs this up: winning posts open on a concrete scene, and abstract maxims lose for every account.

Third, they vet before they connect. Before accepting a connection or replying to a message, they check your headline, your About section, what you've pinned to Featured, and who you know in common. Right now your headline is a list of 16 labels and Featured is empty, so part of this plan is a profile rebuild that holds up to that scrutiny.

Fourth, they follow trusted names. When a peer they respect comments on or shares your post, that works as an introduction. That's why the plan includes you commenting thoughtfully on the posts of people in this circle, and tagging named partners where appropriate.

The benchmark fact at the bottom applies across all five accounts we studied: posts of 150 to 300 words and early morning timing, around 7 to 8am local, consistently perform best.

## Slide 5: The Three-Pillar Content Strategy

**Visual Content:**

- Engagement: the human side, contrarian takes | Measured by comments
- Growth: same-morning market reactions, video from the room, daily comments on peer posts | Measured by new followers
- Positioning: capital and deal mechanics, luxury and celebration economy, policy read like a term sheet | Measured by HNWI DMs
- All three: Operator's War Room, a weekly deal story series ("From the deal room.")

**Speaker Notes (For Jillian):**

Every piece of content maps to one of three goals: engagement, growth or positioning. If a draft doesn't clearly serve at least one, it doesn't go out.

Engagement is about starting conversations. The two themes here are the human side, meaning personal stories, character and origin moments, and contrarian takes that invite respectful debate. In the benchmark data, personal posts are consistently among the top performers, and Harry Stebbings drives his comments by openly inviting pushback. We measure this pillar by comments, because comments are the signal LinkedIn rewards most and right now you average half a comment per post.

Growth is about reaching new people beyond your existing network. Three levers: same-morning reactions when the RBA, a budget or a major market move happens, which is Bouris's highest-yield format; short video from the room at events and briefings, which is how Jauncey gets most of his best results and was your own best post; and you commenting thoughtfully each day on posts from people in the HNWI circle. We measure this by new followers, and especially new followers from the target groups.

Positioning is about being known for something specific. Three themes: capital and deal mechanics, explaining how deals are really structured; the luxury and celebration economy, which ties to Wonder Group; and policy read like a term sheet, what it charges, what it caps and who captures the value. We measure this by the quality signals: direct messages, introductions and invitations from HNWIs and their advisers.

The band at the bottom is the one format that does all three: the Operator's War Room series. Every week, a dated deal story with the same opener, something like "From the deal room." It follows a set structure: the scene, the number, the clause, three lessons, then a one-line close. It drives comments, it gets shared, and it proves your credentials without bragging.

## Slide 6: Content Cadence & Production Rhythm

**Visual Content:**

- Mon 8am: Operator's War Room [E][G][P]
- Wed 8am: Capital or luxury insight [P][G]
- Fri 8am: The human side [E][G]
- Pipeline: Story Bank > Draft > Review > Schedule > Engage
- 150 to 300 words · Fortnightly: 1 video + 1 teardown · 2 to 3 posts always banked

**Speaker Notes (For Jillian):**

Consistency was the single biggest finding in the benchmark study. Every one of the five accounts posts on a fixed rhythm, and none of them goes quiet for weeks. You posted 11 times in the last six months and nothing since 19 August, so this is the most important change we can make.

The proposal is three posts a week, Monday, Wednesday and Friday at 8am Sydney time. That's Adam Geha's exact schedule. It's sustainable, it's predictable for both the algorithm and your audience, and early morning is when senior readers are on LinkedIn.

Each day has a job. Monday is the Operator's War Room deal story, which serves all three goals. Wednesday is a capital or luxury market insight, for positioning and growth. When something big happens, like a rate decision or a budget, Wednesday's slot can become a same-morning reaction. Friday is the human side, a personal story, for engagement and growth.

Behind the scenes, every post moves through the same five steps: from the Story Bank, to my draft, to your review, to scheduling, and then engagement once it's live, which is where you reply to comments in the first hour.

A few standards across the board. Every post is 150 to 300 words, which is the range that wins across the benchmark accounts. Every fortnight we aim for one short video from the room and one teardown of a deal, market or policy. And I always keep two or three timeless posts in reserve, so a busy week for you never means a gap in the feed.

Over 90 days, this rhythm delivers 36 posts. I'd rather we hit three a week reliably than aim for five and stall.
