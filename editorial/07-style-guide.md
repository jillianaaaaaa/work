# 07 Style Guide

A luxury voice is the quality of the decisions behind the writing, not a vocabulary of sophisticated words. This guide is operational: consult it while writing. For article mechanics (structure, banned words, punctuation rules, CTA block, links, photos), the `wedded-wonderland-articles` skill is binding and is not repeated here.

## 1. Positioning and audience

Wedded Wonderland is a luxury editorial authority on destination weddings, celebrations and the travel around them. Our reader is a discerning, high-net-worth couple (and the planners and families around them) who value privacy, specificity and access, and who want informed judgement more than inspiration alone. Our partners are venues, hotels, planners and creative professionals who value being selected by a publication whose selection carries weight.

## 2. Tone principles

| Principle | Means | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Precision | Specific details instead of broad praise | "Beautiful", "amazing", unnamed settings |
| Restraint | Fewer adjectives, stronger evidence | Stacked adjectives; superlatives without proof |
| Authority | Informed statements without self-importance | Hedging, or boasting about our own expertise |
| Warmth | Human understanding without sentimental excess | "Dream day", "fairytale", over-emotional framing |
| Distinction | Language that reflects the place's actual character | Copy that would fit any property if the name were swapped |
| Clarity | Useful information presented elegantly | Style that obscures practical facts |

### Approved and rejected examples

Rejected: "Discover an exquisite and exclusive destination offering unparalleled luxury for the wedding of your dreams."

Approved: "Set among the hills above Lake Como, the estate offers a private setting for long-table dinners, garden ceremonies and celebrations shaped around the landscape."

Why: the second gives a setting, a sense of the experience and a reason to consider the property. It persuades because it informs.

Rejected: "This iconic hotel is the ultimate choice for the most discerning couples."
Approved: "With exclusive use of its 28 rooms available from November to March, the hotel can host a full wedding party under one roof." (Facts illustrative; verify before use.)

## 3. Vocabulary

Prefer: named places, materials, architects, producers, rituals, dining formats, seasons, capacities.

Avoid as empty signals: exquisite, unparalleled, iconic, ultimate, world-class, once-in-a-lifetime, hidden gem, paradise, fairytale, dream wedding, luxury and exclusive used as decoration. Use "exclusive" only for a verified fact (exclusive use, exclusive access). Also apply the skill's banned words list in full.

## 4. Australian English and house style

Australian spelling (colour, organise, travelled, centre), AUD for currency, numbers as the skill specifies. Dates as 21 October 2026. Place names in their common English form with local names where they help ("Lake Como (Lago di Como)" on first use only when useful).

## 5. Partner claims, sponsorship and superlatives

- Every superlative needs evidence we can cite ("the oldest", "the only") or it is removed.
- Partner-supplied claims are verified or attributed; unverifiable claims are cut.
- Commercial relationships are disclosed clearly in the format the CMS supports. Disclosure does not lower the editorial standard.
- Partners do not approve editorial copy; they may correct facts.

## 5a. Sources we cite (Jillian, 21 Oct 2026)

- News, policy, legal and government topics: credible, established, trusted news outlets and primary sources only. Never Axios or any AI-driven or aggregator outlet.
- Fashion, beauty, celebrity and wedding topics: the most authoritative established source (Vogue may be cited; official brand and designer channels).
- Never a direct competitor (wedding directories and planning sites), including statistics an outlet borrows from one. If a figure traces only to a competitor, write the trend qualitatively instead.
- Titles of cited works are quoted exactly, even when they contain words we would not use ourselves.

## 5b. Hospitality voice (Jillian, 21 Oct 2026)

We write as hosts. Reader-facing copy, and FAQ answers above all, describes how things are handled and reassures the couple that they are looked after; Wedded Concierge carries the logistics. We never hand the reader a to-do list ("make sure you book", "ask the venue", "confirm", "check").

Rejected: "Ask the venue for its terms in writing before you sign."
Approved: "We secure each venue's terms for your dates in writing, so nothing about the weekend is left to assumption."

Internal frameworks (the six criteria, "tests", classifications) never appear as jargon in reader copy.

## 5c. Destinations, discreetly (Jillian, 21 Oct 2026)

Take every natural opportunity to mention one or two of our top destinations (Lake Como, Tuscany, the Amalfi Coast, France), linked to their pages. Implied, never a hard sell, never a claim we have not verified.

## 6. SEO and AI-search principles

See `09-seo-ai-discoverability.md`. In short: clear answers first, verifiable expertise, specific information, consistent structure.

## 7. Listings, destination terminology and naming

- Destination names follow the hierarchy in `05-destination-authority.md` and the site's existing URLs.
- Property names as the property styles them officially, on first use; a short form after.
- Listing structure and fields: `08-listing-standard.md`.

## 8. Images, licensing, credits and visual identity

- Images must show what the copy claims. Landscape first, editorial grade, no third-party watermarks.
- Every image has a credit line "Source: [Official name]" and a recorded permission basis.
- Visual identity: natural light, real settings, people and tables over empty renders; avoid heavy filters and stock cliches.
- Full sourcing order and flags: the skill's Photos and embeds section.

## 9. Templates for recurring formats

All recurring formats have templates in `templates/`; article category and format skeletons live in the skill. If a format is used three times without a template, create one.

## 10. Corrections and review schedule

**Corrections.** Factual errors are corrected promptly. Material corrections (legal, fees, capacity, safety, a misattributed claim) carry a dated correction note on the page. Minor fixes (typos, broken links) are made silently. Every correction is logged in the asset's `status.md` and, if it affects a dossier or listing, there too.

**Review schedule.**

| Content | Review interval |
|---|---|
| Legal requirements, fees, entry rules | Every 6 months, and before any reuse |
| Listings | Every 12 months, or on notice of change |
| Destination hubs and dossiers | Every 12 months; seasonal sections before each season |
| Trend and news pieces | At 6 to 8 weeks (refresh loop), then annually or retire |
| Evergreen guides | Every 12 months |
