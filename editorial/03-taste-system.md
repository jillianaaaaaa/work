# 03 The Taste System

Taste cannot be reduced to a checklist, but it can be taught, documented and applied consistently. This framework decides what belongs on the platform.

## The six criteria

| Criterion | Editorial question | Evidence that counts |
|---|---|---|
| Design integrity | Is there a clear design language, architectural quality or thoughtful execution? | Named architect or designer, period and restoration story, coherent materials and interiors, considered service design |
| Distinctiveness | What makes this meaningfully different from comparable options? | A specific feature no nearby competitor offers; a format, setting or access that is rare |
| Experience | What can a couple and their guests actually experience here? | Ceremony and reception settings, dining formats, multi-day possibilities, guest activities, verified |
| Cultural and local relevance | Does it offer a genuine connection to its destination, people or traditions? | Local cuisine and producers, craft, heritage, community, landscape that shapes the celebration |
| Audience relevance | Does it serve the needs and aspirations of the couples Wedded Wonderland speaks to? | Privacy, exclusivity of use, guest capacity range, accommodation for guests, travel access |
| Editorial evidence | Can its defining qualities be verified through reliable sources, firsthand access or credible documentation? | Official sources, site visits, credible press, expert testimony |

**How to use them.** They guide selection; they are not a formula. A property need not excel in all six. Rate each Strong, Present, Weak or Unknown in `templates/taste-assessment.md`, then make a judgement in words. Two rules are firm:

- **Editorial evidence cannot be Weak or Unknown** for a published recommendation. Without evidence we cannot make the claim.
- **Distinctiveness cannot be Weak** for Signature.

## The three classifications

| Classification | Meaning | Typical profile | Editorial treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Signature** | The definitive shortlist | Exceptional on most criteria, Strong distinctiveness and evidence, embodies the brand's standards | Deeper coverage: features, destination hub placement, priority in lists, first for photography and interviews |
| **Specialist** | The right choice for a particular need | Strong for a specific location, format, guest count, architectural style or travel preference | Listed and recommended for that need, with the need stated explicitly ("for celebrations of under 40 guests") |
| **Emerging** | Worth watching | New openings, rising creative talent, developing ideas with real potential | Clearly labelled as emerging; never presented as an established recommendation; re-assessed within 12 months |
| Decline | Not for us now | Fails evidence, or offers nothing distinctive to our audience | Logged with the reason so the decision is consistent next time |

This hierarchy lets the team cover a broad market without treating every listing as equally exceptional.

## Partners

Commercial partners go through the same assessment. A partnership can determine **what** we cover and **when**; it never determines the classification. If a partner would be Decline, the editorial lead decides whether a different, honest format is possible (for example a disclosed partner announcement) or whether we decline.

## Where decisions are recorded

- The classification and reason go into the listing record (`knowledge/listings/`) and the relevant dossier's venue ecosystem.
- Declines are recorded in the dossier too, with the date, so we do not reassess the same property from scratch.
