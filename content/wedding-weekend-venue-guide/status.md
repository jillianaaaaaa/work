## Why this, why now (editorial lead)

Nearly one in three weddings now runs three days or more (independently reported by Axios, 18 Oct 2026) and this season's most documented luxury weddings unfolded over a weekend on a single estate, so the venue decision has quietly changed from "where is the ceremony" to "which property can host the whole itinerary". No one is telling couples how to read a venue for that, and it is exactly the judgement our destination and Concierge expertise exists to supply.

## Editorial angle

When a wedding becomes a three-day itinerary, the right venue is no longer the most beautiful ceremony setting but the property that can host every occasion and every guest without the weekend fragmenting across town. We show couples how to read a hotel, estate or borgo for a wedding weekend: how much of it they can take, how many distinct settings it offers for each occasion, where guests sleep, and how everyone moves between events.

(Writer note: the first sentence above uses a contrast for internal clarity only; the draft must not use "X, not Y" framing.)

## The three questions (draft answers)

1. **Why does this deserve to be featured?** The multi-day wedding is now a measurable, independently reported shift, and it changes the single most consequential decision a destination couple makes, the venue.
2. **What can our reader learn here that a generic search result would not give them?** A planner's way of reading a property for a weekend (buy-out terms, number of distinct event settings, guest room inventory, distances between events, where the legal ceremony fits), shown through three or four named, verified estates. Search results currently offer either trend round-ups or single-event etiquette.
3. **Does this strengthen Wedded Wonderland's authority and point of view?** Yes, if it reads as destination and venue expertise and leads naturally to /destination-wedding-packages, /plan-your-destination-wedding, /wonderland-concierge and the regional hubs (/tuscany-wedding, /lake-como-wedding, /europe/italy). It will be weak if the worked examples rest on press descriptions instead of verified venue facts, so stage 02 must deliver primary-source facts.

## What this piece must NOT become

- **An event-by-event weekend guide.** We already publish the welcome party, rehearsal dinner, recovery brunch and morning-after pieces, plus "How To Turn Your Destination Wedding Event Into An Experience" (Dec 2023, updated Aug 2026) and the Sofia Franklyn Lake Como and Isabelle and Johnny Newport weekend stories. Link to them; do not re-answer them. Every H2 must be about the venue and the hosting.
- **The two-ceremony story.** The separate legal ceremony is covered in the Angel Dei "Second Wedding Era" section and the Amalfi venues guide. Mention it only as one occasion the venue must accommodate, and link.
- **A wardrobe or "five looks" piece.** Opportunity C is declined as a standalone; the style desk already covers welcome dinner looks, second dresses and morning-after outfits.
- **A Dove Cameron recap.** We already have that article (which needs its own update). Castiglion del Bosco is a worked example of a venue, not the subject.
- **Budget or cost framing.** No "budget", "affordable", "average" or price-led advice. Guest consideration is written as hospitality (what the couple provides and plans for guests who give up several days), never as cost-saving. Any fee carries the skill's disclaimer.
- **A venue listicle with unearned praise.** Each named property needs a taste assessment from ww-editorial-director before it is presented as a recommendation; until then it is a worked example of the criteria, described with verified facts only.
- **A Knot-sourced piece.** The Knot is a competitor: read for the market, never cited. Cite Axios for the figure, or our own Concierge evidence.
