### Slide 5: How the Audience Behaves on LinkedIn
**Visual / On-Slide Content:**
- They read early: 7am to 8am local wins
- They engage with names, dates and numbers, not abstract ideas
- 150 to 300 words beats shorter and longer
- Native posts win; links, carousels and promos lose
- Reach is earned in the comments [E]

**Speaker Notes (Jillian):**
Across all five benchmarks, some clear behaviour patterns came through.

Timing: senior audiences read early. Geha posts at 8am Sydney every time, Bouris at 7 to 8am. Your posts have mostly gone out at 11am, irregularly. [Insert regional audience split from analytics.] If a meaningful share of your audience is in the Gulf, we can test a secondary slot for the Middle East later.

What they engage with: posts that open on a concrete scene with a name, a date and a number. "2017. We had $120 million tied up." is your own example and it's exactly right. Posts that open on a thesis, a question or an abstract maxim lose, for every benchmark.

Length: 150 to 300 words is the sweet spot for Geha, Jauncey, Nate and Stebbings. Above 300 words, engagement drops 30% to 65%.

Format: native text, images and short video win. Link-out posts, carousels, event recaps and promotional posts are the weakest content for everyone.

And the most important behaviour: reach comes from comments. Posts that invite genuine pushback, tag named people who reply, or ask a question that costs the reader something to answer, get the comments. That's why part of this plan involves you in the comments, not just me in the drafts.
