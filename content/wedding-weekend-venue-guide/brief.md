# Brief: How to Choose a Wedding Weekend Venue That Hosts Every Occasion

**Tier:** 2 · **Owner:** ww-writer (with ww-seo-strategist) · **Approver:** Jillian (delegated editor may approve at Tier 2) · **Category + format:** Destination weddings + Trending or news (timely hook, venue-reading core built to stay useful after the hook fades)

Prepared 21 October 2026 from `status.md`, `research.md`, `seo.md`, the four featured listing records and the Editorial Director decisions of 21 October 2026. The Checkpoint 1 package is in `outline.md`.

## 1. Editorial angle

When a celebration runs over three days, the venue has to carry the whole stay, and the property that suits it is the one that can host every occasion and every guest in one place. We show couples how to read a hotel, estate or borgo for that job through five tests (privacy, settings, beds, movement, house rules), each proven with dated operator facts from four named properties, which no ranking page currently does.

## 2. Audience and objective

High-net-worth couples planning a destination wedding for roughly 40 to 150 guests, at the shortlisting stage (Comparison moving into Selection), often with a planner or Wedded Concierge, with guests flying in. Afterwards the reader can judge whether a property can hold a welcome dinner, ceremony, celebration and day-after lunch plus every guest, and has the written questions to send each events team before signing.

## 3. The three questions (draft answers)

1. **Why does this deserve to be featured?** The multi-day wedding is now independently reported (Axios, 18 Oct 2026, US data) and matches demand for exclusive-use travel (Virtuoso 2027 Luxe Report: 47 percent of advisors report an increase). It changes the single most consequential decision a destination couple makes.
2. **What will the reader learn here that a generic result would not give them?** How exclusive use is tiered in practice, how to count settings per occasion, how to set beds against guests, how movement and house rules shape the days, each shown with verified, dated facts from properties in England, Italy and South Africa. Ranking pages are US-regional listings or event-by-event guides and lean on cost.
3. **How does this strengthen Wedded Wonderland's authority and point of view?** It reads as venue and destination judgement, makes two clear recommendations (Heckfield Place, Babylonstoren for under 100), uses two properties strictly as evidence, and leads naturally to /plan-your-destination-wedding, /destination-wedding-packages and /wonderland-concierge. It becomes the primary page for "choosing a venue for a multi-day wedding weekend".

- Hospitality, never cost. Guests who give several days are hosted; no price, spend or value language. Fees appear only as "an additional fee" with the change disclaimer.
- Restraint around the celebrity hook: one or two sentences, facts only, no wardrobe, no guest speculation.
- Principle examples (Castiglion del Bosco, Villa Igiea) are described, never recommended. Recommendations (Heckfield, Babylonstoren) say plainly what they suit and where their limit sits.
- "Exclusive" only for verified exclusive-use facts. No decorative superlatives; every number dated to its operator document.
- Answer-first: the first sentence under every H2 and H3 is a quotable answer.
